# Southern New Hampshire University Computer Science Capstone

## Professional Self-Assessment

When I began studying Computer Science at Southern New Hampshire University in September of 2023, I had no idea what to expect. At this point in my life, I'd graduated from the University of Maine (in-person) with a bachelor's degree in Psychology and had worked in the field of psychology for the past six years. I knew I wasn't happy or passionate in the jobs that I was holding, and I've always had a passion for technology that I've been too intimidated to pursue. Once I made the decision to take the leap and start this degree, I truly felt like I was starting over; it was both a terrifying and exciting feeling. I'd never attended a completely online program (disregarding the remote learning that I experienced at the University of Maine due to COVID-19), and my life was at a completely different point than it was when I started my previous degree. Looking back at it now, I'm grateful I took the risk to follow my passion. Although there were countless hours of struggling to grasp concepts, debugging code, and researching solutions, I never lost the passion.

My experiences in this degree have been enlightening, insightful, and challenging. Each assignment taught me valuable lessons in patience and perseverance, which over time, molded me into the computer scientist I am now. The variety in courses that I've completed exposed me to the many avenues of computer science, which broadened my perspective on professional goals and values that I hadn't considered prior. One of the earlier pieces of work that I completed in my degree was a text-based called Fear the Light. This specific project sticks in my mind not only due to the sheer amount of time I spent working on the game, but the creativity it allowed me to express. This was my first real exposure to implementing a narrative to evoke emotions through code, developing an inventory system, and creating conditional events. Since this project allowed for a considerable amount of original thought, I was encouraged to hone my skills to develop the project I envisioned. I learned how to properly implement loops, ensure my code was nested properly, and boundary test. Being someone who's always played video games (and still does), this reinforced my professional goal to become a developer for some aspect of video games. In a different way, another piece of work that has stuck in my mind is a 3D scene I created based on a picture I took. Although this required creativity and originality to create, the reason this project appeals to me is due to the visual presentation. This task connected a lot of psychological concepts with computer science concepts, which I found fascinating. While constructing my scene, I had to ensure the objects dimensions were appropriate, the textures were properly mapped, and the scene's ambience matched the original picture. These were necessary to fulfill the criteria of the assignment, but from my perspective, they were also necessary to avoid producing an uncanny scene. With this experience of blending physical computer science concepts and psychological perspective concepts, this reinforced my goal and value of creating visually-appealing products. With this in mind, I'm fascinated with the impacts of User Interface and User Experience (UI/UX) on human perspective and psychology.

Throughout my experiences in this degree, I've encountered many scenarios where coordination and cooperation is a necessity. Alongside the technical aspects of this degree, there were important real-world skills that needed to be honed like collaborating in a team environment and communicating with stakeholders. One course that highlighted the importance of these real-world skills was my Software Development Lifecycle course. I engaged in a role-play scenario where my teammates and I each held roles in a Scrum-Agile development environment where we were either the Scrum Master, Product Owner, Tester, or Developer. Each role had to understand their responsibilities, communicate effectively with the other team members and/or stakeholders, and be accountable for their actions and presence on the team. When working as a team, each person is only as good as the other. Thus, if one person doesn't complete their responsibilities, the whole team suffers. 

To showcase my technical skills, abilities, and proficiency in computer science, this ePortfolio will highlight a few works throughout my degree that demonstrate industry-standard coding practices, proactive design and coding approaches, and a foundational understanding of a code's purpose. My ePortfolio will consist of a code review and three categories of works: Software Design and Engineering, Algorithms and Data Structures, and Databases. Each category will have a unique, representative artifact that has been enhanced by applying what I've learned throughout this degree to increase its quality. Each enhancement will address different course outcomes, and when viewed as a whole, will provide a comprehensive coverage of the course outcomes and my preparedness to enter the computer science field.

**Course Outcome 1:** Employ strategies for building collaborative environments that enable diverse audiences to support organizational decision making in the field of computer science. The aspects of my ePortfolio that address this course outcome are the code review, the Software Design and Engineering artifact, and the Databases artifact. My code review demonstrates the ability to conduct an efficient code review by understanding the necessities of clean and detailed coding practices while critically analyzing the contents of my own works while minimizing bias. My Software Design and Engineering artifact highlights my focus in developing a collaborative environment by enabling user engagement and creating layouts that support multiple mobile devices. My Databases artifact is a Dash application that shows a data table, a pie chart, and a geographical map that displays information regarding the animals in an animal shelter's database. This artifact shows my understanding of working with multiple interconnected components to create an organized, customizable product that can be applied to different companies, products, and data.

**Course Outcome 2:** Design, develop, and deliver professional-quality oral, written, and visual communications that are coherent, technically sound, and appropriately adapted to specific audiences and contexts. The aspects of my ePortfolio that address this course outcome are the code review and the Software Design and Engineering artifact. My code review presents the initial artifacts in a coherent, comprehensive manner that address the technical aspects and maintainability of each artifact. My Software Design and Engineering artifact presents a mobile application that delivers professional visual communications and is designed to be a quick, reliable, and stable application that can be used by various audiences.

**Course Outcome 3:** Design and evaluate computing solutions that solve a given problem using algorithmic principles and computer science practices and standards appropriate to its solution, while managing the trade-offs involved in design choices. The aspect of my ePortfolio that address this course outcome is the Algorithms and Data Structures artifact. In this artifact, I translated JUnit tests (utilizing the Java language) into Google Tests (utilizing the C++ language). In this artifact, the trade-offs of conducting unit tests in a different language had to be observed, understood, and managed. The algorithmic principles and computer science practices and standards were effectively implemented when designing the unit tests in a different Integrated Development Environment (IDE) and language.

**Course Outcome 4:** Demonstrate an ability to use well-founded and innovative techniques, skills, and tools in computing practices for the purpose of implementing computer solutions that deliver value and accomplish industry-specific goals. The aspects of my ePortfolio that address this course outcome are the Software Design and Engineering artifact, the Algorithms and Data Structures artifact, and the Databases artifact. My Software Design and Engineering artifact demonstrates my innovate techniques for screen navigation, permission handling, and theme customization. My Algorithms and Data Structures artifact show my core understanding of unit testing and the ability to translate my skills in development from one language to another while remaining proficient across multiple IDEs. My Databases artifact shows my ability to display data in multiple innovative methods while maintaining a robust functionality of the code.

**Course Outcome 5:** Develop a security mindset that anticipates adversarial exploits in software architecture and designs to expose potential vulnerabilities, mitigate design flaws, and ensure privacy and enhanced security of data and resources. The aspect of my ePortfolio that address this course outcome is the Databases artifact. In this artifact, I determined where vulnerabilities were present in my code, provided local solutions for security vulnerabilities, and outlined the functionality of my code chunks so maintenance can occur without unintended effects.
